# Results

Generated by `python src/train.py`. All figures are on the held-out test set,
which was split off before any model was fitted and used exactly once.

## Class balance

45,211 customers, of whom 5,289 subscribed (11.7%).
Predicting "no" for everyone therefore scores **88.3% accuracy** while
finding nobody.

## Model comparison

| Model | Threshold | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | PR-AUC | Cost of mistakes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline — always predicts 'no' | 0.50 | 88.3% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.117 | 2,116,000 |
| Logistic regression, default threshold | 0.50 | 89.3% | 66.7% | 17.6% | 0.411 | 1,753,300 |
| Logistic regression, tuned threshold | 0.04 | 30.7% | 13.9% | 94.5% | 0.411 | 736,900 |
| Gradient boosting, tuned threshold | 0.06 | 52.9% | 18.1% | 85.5% | 0.457 | 716,200 |

Winner on business cost: **gradient boosting**.

## Data leakage

`duration` (length of the sales call in seconds) is not known before the call is
made, so it cannot be an input to a decision about whom to call. On its own it
scores ROC-AUC **0.807** against the target.

| | Recall | Precision | PR-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| With `duration` | 34.5% | 64.5% | 0.545 |
| Without `duration` | 17.6% | 66.7% | 0.411 |

## Stability

5-fold cross-validated PR-AUC on the training data:
**0.400 +/- 0.034**, against a test-set PR-AUC of
0.411.
